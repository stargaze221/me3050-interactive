One idea before the formula

A useful intuition is to treat a transform as a correlation-like comparison: multiply the signal by a test pattern and accumulate the result over time.

Step 2. Multiply and accumulate

The real channel multiplies the signal by e−σt cos(ωt). Positive and negative contributions can reinforce or cancel as they are accumulated.

Product Running integral
The complete Laplace value is complex. A second quadrature channel uses −e−σt sin(ωt); together the two channels form FT(s).

Step 3. Scan across frequency

Hold σ fixed and scan ω. The magnitude |FT(σ + jω)| becomes larger where the signal has stronger oscillatory agreement with the test pattern.

From Fourier intuition to Laplace

Set σ = 0

e−st = e−jωt

Only the oscillatory comparison remains. This is the Fourier-like viewpoint.

Allow σ > 0

e−st = e−σte−jωt

Now the comparison also includes exponential time weighting. This leads to the Laplace viewpoint.

Laplace transform
F(s) = ∫0∞ f(t)e−st dt,   s = σ + jω
Engineering payoff: this transform is not only a way to represent signals. For linear dynamic systems it turns derivatives into algebraic terms, for example ℒ{ẋ(t)} = sX(s) − x(0).
