2. Control effort

Better tracking is not free. More aggressive gains can demand larger actuator commands and can even destabilize the closed loop.

3. What each term contributes

P

Proportional: change stiffness and speed

Increasing Kc usually makes tracking faster and reduces offset, but it can also increase overshoot. Without integral action, a constant step generally retains steady-state error.

I

Integral: remove offset, but add a pole

Integral action drives constant tracking error toward zero. In a second-order plant it also raises the closed-loop order, so too much Ki can create severe oscillation or instability.

D

Derivative: add damping

Derivative action reacts to output rate and often suppresses overshoot. Here derivative is taken on the measured output to avoid a derivative kick from a step command.

4. A tuning experiment

Try this sequence: start with the lightly damped plant at ζ = 0.25. Compare P and PD to see derivative damping directly. Then use PI to eliminate offset. Finally switch to PID and balance speed, overshoot, and control effort. Finish with Too much I to see that integral action is not automatically stabilizing.
