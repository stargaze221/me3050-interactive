5. What each term contributes

P

Proportional: change stiffness and speed

Increasing Kc usually makes tracking faster and reduces offset, but it can also increase overshoot. On the root locus, changing proportional action shifts the controller shape and therefore the pole paths.

I

Integral: remove offset, but add a pole

Integral action drives constant tracking error toward zero. It also adds an open-loop pole at the origin, changes the root locus, and can sharply reduce stability margin if Ki is too large.

D

Derivative: add damping and phase lead

Derivative action reacts to output rate, often moving closed-loop poles toward better damping. In the frequency domain it can add phase lead and increase phase margin.

6. A tuning experiment

Try this sequence: start with ζ = 0.25. Compare P and PD while watching the root locus and phase margin. Then use PI to eliminate offset and notice the added pole at the origin. Finally switch to PID and balance speed, overshoot, control effort, and stability margin. Finish with Too much I and watch the root locus cross into the right-half plane while the margins deteriorate.
