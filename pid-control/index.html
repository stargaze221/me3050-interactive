2. Control effort

Better tracking is not free. More aggressive gains often demand a larger or faster actuator command.

3. What each term contributes

P

Proportional: react to present error

Increasing Kc generally speeds the response and reduces offset, but P-only control of this plant still has nonzero steady-state error.

I

Integral: remember accumulated error

Integral action drives constant step-tracking error toward zero, but too much Ki can increase overshoot and oscillatory behavior.

D

Derivative: respond to motion

Derivative action adds damping by reacting to output rate, often reducing overshoot while increasing sensitivity to rapid measurement changes.

4. A tuning experiment

Try this sequence: select P and adjust Kc. Then switch to PI and increase Ki until the offset disappears at an acceptable speed. Finally add Kd and see whether you can reduce overshoot without slowing the response too much.
